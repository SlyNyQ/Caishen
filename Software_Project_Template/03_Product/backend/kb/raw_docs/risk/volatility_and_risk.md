# Volatility And Risk

Volatility measures the size and frequency of price movements. It describes variation, not direction, and is commonly annualized from daily returns. High volatility can create both larger gains and larger losses.

Risk is broader than volatility. Important risks include permanent capital loss, concentration, liquidity, valuation, business-model change, leverage, currency exposure, and the possibility that available information is incomplete.

Comparisons should pair performance with volatility and drawdown context. A short observation window can produce misleading conclusions, especially after unusual market events.

Risk tolerance describes an investor's ability and willingness to absorb losses. Caishen uses a stated risk tolerance only to frame educational scenarios; it does not determine suitability or issue personalized advice.
